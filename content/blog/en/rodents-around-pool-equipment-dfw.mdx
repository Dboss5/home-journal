A pool heater cabinet or a sheltered equipment pad can attract attention when you find nesting material or droppings nearby. The important next step is to protect people, document the location, and arrange the right inspection. A chewed cable, a nest, and a noisy pump can require different specialists.

## Observe without opening or disturbing

Look from accessible ground and photograph visible signs from a distance. Note where material appears, whether it returns, and whether the equipment has displayed errors or stopped working. Keep children and pets away from the affected area.

Do not open energized equipment, reach inside a heater, or disturb droppings to identify them. Avoid dry sweeping or vacuuming contaminated material. Ask the pest professional for appropriate cleanup guidance and whether a separate cleanup service is needed.

## Review the habitat around the equipment

Remove loose storage and avoid stacking unused materials against cabinets. Keep access paths clear and inspect vegetation near the pad. Store pet food and birdseed in secure containers and address accessible food waste.

Look for moisture, but do not assume the pool is leaking. Irrigation and household pipes can also leave damp ground. Our [pool or plumbing leak guide](/en/blog/pool-or-plumbing-leak-dfw) explains how to record the clues before choosing an inspection.

## Treat exclusion and equipment repair as separate jobs

A pest specialist can assess activity, likely travel routes, and suitable exclusion measures. [Supremacy Service's rodent control and exclusion page](https://supremacyservice.com/rodent-control-and-exclusion-dfw/) is a relevant starting point for that discussion.

An equipment technician should inspect suspected damage to pool controls, heater components, or associated wiring. [Poolburg's article about rodents in pool heaters](https://poolburg.com/rodents-in-pool-heaters-a-hidden-threat-to-your-equipment-and-how-to-stop-it/) identifies the equipment context for a pool service conversation. Finding pest activity does not establish that every operating problem was caused by it.

Ask both providers who will confirm the cabinet is safe to operate, who handles cleanup, and whether inspection should happen before exclusion work. Keep photos and reports together.

## Do not seal every opening

Heaters and other equipment need specified ventilation, drainage, and service access. Improvised mesh, expanding foam, or covers can obstruct required openings or interfere with safe operation. Any protective alteration should follow the manufacturer's instructions and be checked by the appropriate professional.

Similarly, blocking an entry point without a plan can leave animals inside a structure or cabinet. Let a qualified provider determine the sequence for removal, exclusion, and repairs. Avoid placing poison, traps, or pesticides inside equipment as a DIY experiment.

## Ask for a complete scope

A useful proposal distinguishes inspection, removal or treatment, exclusion materials, cleanup, and follow-up. Ask whether inaccessible areas are excluded and how recurrent activity will be assessed. If wiring or insulation needs repair, request a separate equipment or electrical scope rather than assuming the pest visit covers it.

Before restarting equipment that may be damaged, obtain clearance from the appropriate technician. If you notice smoke, sparking, or a strong burning smell, stay clear and seek urgent help.
