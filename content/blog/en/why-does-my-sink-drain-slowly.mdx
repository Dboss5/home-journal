**A slow sink may have buildup near the fixture or a restriction farther along the drainage system.** One slow basin and several fixtures backing up are different situations. Identify the pattern before paying for a particular cleaning method.

## Is only one fixture affected?

Note which sinks drain slowly and whether toilets, showers or nearby fixtures also gurgle or back up. Record whether the issue is constant, follows heavy use or returned soon after previous cleaning. Do not keep running water to test a fixture that is close to overflowing.

Avoid adding drain chemicals, especially if another product was already used. Tell the plumber exactly what entered the drain, including brand and timing, so the work can be planned safely.

## Could kitchen habits contribute?

Grease and food waste can contribute to buildup. The [City of Portland’s fats, oils and grease guidance](https://www.portland.gov/bes/preventing-pollution/fats-oil-and-grease) explains why pouring grease down a drain creates problems; the prevention principle is useful beyond that city.

A prevention change does not remove an existing obstruction. [Supremacy’s drain-cleaning page](https://supremacyservice.com/drain-cleaning/) is the relevant service resource for discussing the affected fixture, access and suitable cleaning approach.

## When is a wider inspection appropriate?

If several fixtures are affected, backups recur or clearing the sink does not resolve the pattern, ask whether further investigation is justified. [Supremacy’s sewer-line service page](https://supremacyservice.com/sewer-line-repair-and-replacement/) provides context if findings point beyond the local branch, not proof that the sewer needs replacement.

Request an explanation of the likely location, any camera findings and how drainage will be tested after work. Keep the report if the problem returns.

### Should I use boiling water to clear it?

Do not assume every fixture or pipe material is suitable for that approach. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions and seek advice rather than improvising.

### Does one slow sink mean a damaged sewer line?

No. Local causes are possible. A larger repair recommendation should be supported by inspection findings, not the symptom alone.
