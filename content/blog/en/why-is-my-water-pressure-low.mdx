**Weak flow at one fixture and a sudden change throughout the home do not point to the same diagnosis.** Fixture restrictions, supply conditions or a plumbing fault may be involved. Low pressure alone does not prove that the underground water line needs replacement.

## Where is the change noticeable?

Record whether hot, cold or both supplies are affected, and whether the pattern involves one tap or several rooms. Note when it began, recent plumbing work and whether the change appears only when multiple fixtures run.

Check your own water utility’s current notices. [Fort Worth’s water-main guidance](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/water/main-breaks) illustrates how supply problems can affect service; residents elsewhere should contact their actual provider rather than assume the same event applies to their city.

## What should I avoid adjusting?

Do not alter a pressure regulator, operate utility-owned valves or dig to look for a fault. If water is actively escaping, use a known, safely accessible household shutoff when appropriate and obtain help. Keep away from flooded electrical equipment and unstable ground.

[Supremacy’s water-line repair resource](https://supremacyservice.com/water-line-repair-and-replacement-in-dallas/) is relevant when an inspection indicates a supply-line issue. Ask what measurements distinguish pressure, flow and a restriction before agreeing to excavation.

## When should leaks be investigated?

Report unexplained water use, persistent damp ground or a change after recent repairs. These clues may justify [Supremacy’s leak-detection service](https://supremacyservice.com/water-leak-detection/), but none alone locates a buried leak.

Request the findings, where the issue appears to lie and whether the utility or the property owner must take the next step. Responsibility depends on the local service arrangement, so confirm it directly.

### Does weak flow always mean low supply pressure?

No. A restriction at the fixture can reduce flow even when supply pressure is adequate. Describe both the location and timing.

### Should I replace the water line first?

No. Establish the cause and the proposed repair scope. Ask for evidence supporting a major replacement rather than treating it as a diagnostic test.
