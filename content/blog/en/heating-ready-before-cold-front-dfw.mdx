The best time to discover a heating problem is while you still have time to arrange an inspection. A short trial run and a clear record of symptoms can make a service appointment more useful than a rushed call after the house has already cooled down.

This checklist covers observation and routine user checks. It does not require removing equipment covers, adjusting gas components, or working on wiring.

## Confirm the simple settings first

Check that the thermostat is in heating mode, the schedule matches your plans, and the selected temperature calls for heat. If the display is blank, consult the thermostat's user instructions rather than opening electrical compartments. Record any error message before changing settings.

Inspect the air filter and follow the equipment and filter manufacturer's replacement guidance. Keep returns and supply grilles clear of furniture. A dirty filter or blocked grille can affect airflow, but finding one does not rule out another fault.

## Give the technician a useful symptom record

Note whether the system fails to start, runs briefly and stops, or operates while a particular room stays cold. Record the thermostat setting and approximate indoor temperature. Mention recent repairs, power interruptions, or new thermostat installation.

Heat pumps can behave differently from gas furnaces, including changes during defrost or auxiliary heating. Ask a technician to interpret the pattern for your model. Avoid repeatedly resetting a unit that is showing a fault.

Maintenance and fault diagnosis may involve different work.

## Plan around the rest of the property

A cold front also affects exposed plumbing and pool equipment. Review equipment-specific freeze instructions before temperatures fall, and decide who checks the property if you are away. [Poolburg's freeze-protection resource](https://poolburg.com/pool-freeze-protection-north-texas/) can help frame those questions.

Freeze automation is not a backup power source. If power fails, contact your provider for model-specific instructions rather than improvising changes to valves or wiring. If thawing reveals unexplained water, use the observations in [our pool versus plumbing leak guide](/en/blog/pool-or-plumbing-leak-dfw).

## Know when to stop checking

If you smell gas, leave the area and contact the gas utility or emergency services from a safe place. Do not operate switches or attempt a restart. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and contact emergency services. Smoke, sparking, or a burning electrical smell also calls for urgent professional attention.

Keep outdoor combustion exhausts unobstructed and use carbon monoxide alarms according to their instructions. Do not use an oven, grill, or outdoor heater to heat the home.

## Ask for a decision you can review

Request the diagnosis, the proposed repair, and any limits on the estimate. If replacement is recommended, ask which findings make repair unsuitable and how the proposed system fits the property. Avoid deciding from equipment age alone. Our [HVAC repair cost guide](/en/blog/hvac-repair-cost-dfw) provides estimate questions; obtain current pricing for your actual system.
