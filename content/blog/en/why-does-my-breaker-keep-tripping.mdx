**A breaker trip means a protective device interrupted the circuit.** An overloaded circuit, a faulty appliance, damaged wiring or another problem may be involved. Replacing the breaker without identifying the cause can leave the hazard unresolved.

## What should I do when it happens again?

Stop using the affected circuit and arrange an electrical assessment. Do not hold a breaker on, fit a higher-rated breaker or repeatedly reset it to keep an appliance running. Keep clear of equipment with heat damage, buzzing, moisture or a burning smell.

If there is smoke, fire or immediate danger, leave the area and contact emergency services. Do not remove the panel cover to investigate.

## What information helps the electrician?

Write down what was operating when the trip occurred, whether it happens immediately or after a delay, and whether recent changes added equipment. Note which rooms or outlets lost power. If the device has a visible label or indicator, photograph it without touching exposed parts.

The [CPSC home-wiring hazard guide](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/518.pdf) explains why repeated electrical warning signs deserve attention. ## When does breaker replacement make sense?

Ask what testing supports the finding and whether the circuit load, wiring or connected appliance needs correction. A protective device that is doing its job should not be treated as the problem merely because it trips.

If replacement is recommended, use [Supremacy’s breaker installation and replacement page](https://supremacyservice.com/electrical-breaker-installation-or-replacement-in-dallas/) to discuss compatibility with the panel and the proposed work. Request a written scope and ask how the circuit will be checked afterward. A larger breaker is not a substitute for a properly designed circuit.

### Could one appliance be responsible?

Yes, a pattern involving one appliance is useful information. Stop using suspect equipment and report the pattern rather than testing it repeatedly on other outlets.

### Is a GFCI or AFCI trip the same as an overload?

Not necessarily. Different devices detect different conditions. Report the exact device that operated so the electrician can investigate the correct protection function.
