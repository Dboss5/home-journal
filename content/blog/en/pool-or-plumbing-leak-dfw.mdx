A wet patch beside the pool does not automatically mean a pool pipe has failed. Irrigation, rainfall, drainage, household plumbing, and pool equipment can produce overlapping symptoms. The goal of an initial check is to narrow the conversation with a professional without digging or dismantling equipment.

## Write down what changed

Note when you first saw the wet area, when irrigation last ran, and whether the pool has an automatic fill device. Record unusual refilling and any visible drips. Photograph the same location at different times. A pattern is more useful than one isolated photograph after rain.

Compare water bills over similar billing periods. A higher bill can reflect irrigation, guests, or a longer billing cycle as well as leakage. Treat it as a reason to investigate, not proof of a particular fault.

## What the water meter can and cannot tell you

If you can safely read the meter, a plumber may ask you to observe it while known water uses are stopped. Include irrigation, ice makers, water treatment equipment, and automatic pool filling in that conversation. Follow local utility instructions and do not operate utility-owned valves.

Unexpected movement may indicate an unaccounted-for use or a supply-side leak. It cannot locate the problem, identify a sewer leak, or prove that a pool shell is sound. Hidden automatic filling can also disguise water loss from the pool.

## Record the pool level without changing equipment settings

Choose a fixed visual reference and compare readings under similar conditions. Weather, splashing, water features, and refilling all affect the result. Ask a pool technician whether a controlled comparison test is appropriate. Do not disable necessary circulation or freeze protection just to test a theory.

## Match the observation to the inspection

- **Water inside the home:** record the affected fixture or room and whether use triggers the leak. Avoid wet electrical equipment.
- **Wet ground along a possible supply route:** ask a plumber about locating the line and confirming the source before excavation.
- **Repeated pool refilling:** give the pool specialist controller details, fill settings, and level observations.
- **Persistent dampness after rain:** discuss drainage and irrigation rather than assuming pressurized plumbing has failed.

Overlapping clues may require both a plumber and a pool leak specialist. Ask them to agree on the investigation sequence so you do not pay for unrelated excavation.

## What to request before approving a repair

Ask where the leak was confirmed, what test supports that conclusion, how access will be created, and what restoration is included. Confirm whether the estimate covers paving, landscaping, equipment reconnection, and final verification. Our [plumbing cost guide](/en/blog/plumbing-cost-dfw) can help you structure the estimate discussion, but a site inspection determines the actual scope.

Do not drain a pool to search for a leak without qualified advice. Ground conditions and pool construction can make draining risky. If water is reaching electrical equipment, stay clear and seek urgent professional help.
