**A falling pool level can reflect evaporation, splash-out, cleaning or a leak.** The useful question is whether the pattern has changed under similar conditions. Repeatedly topping up the pool hides that pattern and can hide the cost of the lost water.

## Is the water loss normal?

There is no single daily loss figure that fits every Dallas–Fort Worth pool. Wind, humidity, water temperature, covers and use all matter. The [EPA’s pool water-efficiency guidance](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/pool-water-efficiency) identifies several routes for water loss; a lower waterline alone does not locate a leak.

Compare observations over a consistent interval. Note rainfall, swimming, backwashing and automatic refill activity. If an autofill masks the change, ask your pool professional how to monitor loss without compromising the equipment’s minimum water level.

## What should I record before calling?

- Photograph the waterline from the same position and note the time.
- Record whether loss appears different during normal pump operation and scheduled off periods.
- Photograph persistent wet ground or visible drips from a safe distance.
- Note recent work around fittings, decking, plumbing or the equipment pad.

Do not drain the pool, dismantle fittings or let the pump run without adequate water. Wet ground can also come from irrigation or drainage, so it is a clue rather than proof. ## When is leak detection worth booking?

Book an inspection when unexplained loss persists, refill demand increases or a wet area keeps returning. For a local service discussion, use [Poolburg’s DFW leak-detection resource](https://poolburg.com/pool-leak-detection-near-me/) and ask which tests are appropriate before agreeing to repairs.

### Does a bucket comparison prove where the leak is?

No. A properly arranged comparison may help separate evaporation from other loss, but it cannot identify a broken pipe or fitting. Ask the technician how rain, autofill and equipment schedules affect the result.

### Should I keep adding water?

Maintain the level required by the equipment while arranging advice, but record additions. An unexplained change deserves investigation rather than an open-ended refill routine.
