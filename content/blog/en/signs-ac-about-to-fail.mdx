Your AC doesn't die suddenly. It gives you warnings for weeks — sometimes months — before it quits. Most homeowners miss them until it's 95° outside and the house is 88°.

Here's what to watch for.

## The 6 warning signs

### 1. It's not cooling like it used to
If the AC runs constantly but the house never quite gets comfortable, something's wrong. Could be refrigerant, could be a failing compressor, could be a clogged filter. All of them get worse.

### 2. Weird noises
Grinding, squealing, rattling, or banging. Any of these mean something is physically wrong — a bearing, a belt, a fan blade. Don't wait.

### 3. Rising energy bills
If your bill jumps 20%+ month-over-month with no explanation, the system is working harder than it should. That's a symptom.

### 4. Weak airflow
Feel the vents. If the air is barely moving, you've got a duct issue, a blower problem, or a blockage. All fixable — if caught early.

### 5. Moisture or leaks around the unit
Refrigerant leaks, condensate drain clogs, or ice buildup. Any of these can cascade into bigger failures.

### 6. It's over 12 years old
Age alone isn't a failure — but combined with any of the above, it's a signal. Most systems last 15–20 years with maintenance. Past 12, every repair is a judgment call.

## What to do when you spot these

**Step one:** Replace the filter. Seriously. A clogged filter mimics half the symptoms above and costs $20 to fix.

**Step two:** If symptoms persist, call a pro. Don't wait for it to fully fail — emergency repairs cost 30–50% more.

**Step three:** Get a written estimate before any work starts. A good tech will walk you through what they found and what it costs to fix.

## Repair or replace?

The industry rule of thumb: **if the repair costs more than 30% of a new unit, and the system is over 12 years old, replace it.**

Why? Because you're not just paying for this repair — you're paying for the next one. And the one after. Aging systems fail in clusters.

If the system is under 10 years old and the repair is straightforward, repair it. Modern units are expensive; don't replace prematurely.

## Prevent it in the first place

Two things:

1. **Replace your filter every 1–3 months** (more often if you have pets)
2. **Get a tune-up every spring** — before you need it

A $150 tune-up catches 80% of failures before they happen.
