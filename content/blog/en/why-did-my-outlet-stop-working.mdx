**A dead outlet could involve a protective device, a connected appliance, or a wiring problem.** It does not automatically mean the receptacle itself has failed.

## What can I check safely?

Note which outlets lost power and whether it happened after rain or when an appliance was connected. Look for visible damage without removing the cover or touching exposed parts.

The [CPSC's home wiring hazards guide](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/518.pdf) identifies warning signs that deserve professional attention. Heat damage, buzzing, shocks, or a burning smell call for stopping use and arranging prompt help. Stay away from wet electrical equipment.

## Should I keep resetting it?

No: repeated tripping needs a diagnosis. Do not bypass protection or replace a breaker with a larger one to restore power. Follow the device manufacturer's user instructions for any permitted checks.

For one affected location, discuss [outlet and switch repair with Supremacy Service](https://supremacyservice.com/outlets-switches-installation-repair/). If several locations are affected or the cause is unclear, ask about an [electrical safety inspection](https://supremacyservice.com/electrical-safety-inspections-dfw/). Describe the timing and affected appliances; let the electrician determine whether the outlet, circuit, or equipment needs repair.
