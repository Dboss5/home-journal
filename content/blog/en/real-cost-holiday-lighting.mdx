Every November, homeowners face the same question: string the lights yourself, or pay someone? The DIY savings look obvious on paper — until you factor in time, ladder risk, and replacement costs.

Let's run the real numbers.

## The DIY path

**Cost:**
- Lights: $150–$400 (mid-grade, first year)
- Clips, extension cords, timer: $50–$100
- Replacement bulbs/strands (each year): $40–$120

**Time:**
- Install: 6–12 hours (first year), 4–8 hours (subsequent years)
- Takedown: 2–4 hours
- Storage and untangling next year: 1–2 hours

**Risk:**
- Ladder falls are the #1 holiday decorating injury
- Roof damage from improper clips
- Electrical issues from overloading circuits

If you value your time at $30/hour, and spend 10 hours per year on lights, that's $300 in time. Plus $300 average in materials. **$600/year, and you did all the work.**

## The pro path

**Cost:**
- Single-story home: $300–$800 (includes install, takedown, storage)
- Two-story home: $800–$2,000
- Commercial property: $2,000+

**Time:**
- Zero. You make one call.

**Risk:**
- Insured. Any damage is on them, not you.

## Where the real math lives

Here's what most homeowners miss: **the cost of a pro is often lower than DIY once you count time.**

- Single-story home, $500 pro service
- DIY equivalent: ~$300 materials + 10 hours of your life
- If your time is worth more than $20/hour, the pro wins

And that's before you count the cost of a fall, a roof repair, or a burnout on the whole thing.

## When DIY makes sense

- Small, easy-to-reach areas (bushes, single-level eaves)
- You genuinely enjoy the process
- You already own commercial-grade lights
- You have a safe way to reach everything without a ladder

## When to hire a pro

- Two-story or taller homes
- Complex roof lines
- Commercial or multi-property
- You want lights that actually look designed
- You've done DIY for years and you're tired of it

## What to ask a pro

1. **Are you insured?** — Get proof. Always.
2. **What's included?** — Install, maintenance mid-season, takedown, storage?
3. **What's the wire strategy?** — Visible wires kill the look. Good pros hide them.
4. **When do you book out?** — Best pros are booked by mid-October. If you're calling in December, you'll pay premium.

## The bottom line

If your time is worth anything, and your house is more than one story, **hire a pro.** The savings from DIY are usually an illusion. What you're really buying is peace of mind, a better result, and 10 hours back in your December.
