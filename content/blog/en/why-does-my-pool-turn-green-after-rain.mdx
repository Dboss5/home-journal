**Rain can coincide with changes in water balance, debris and circulation, but green water is not enough to choose a treatment.** Check the water and equipment before adding products. A storm may expose a problem that was already developing rather than create one simple chemical shortage.

## What changed during the storm?

Write down when the water changed color, whether yard runoff entered the pool and whether power or pump operation was interrupted. Note leaves, fine debris, an overflowing pool or recent chemical additions. These observations help separate a sanitation issue from other causes of discoloration.

[Poolburg’s green-pool guide](https://poolburg.com/why-is-my-pool-green/) explains the service context. Avoid treating every green shade as the same problem: water readings and an inspection are more useful than a photograph alone.

## What should I check before treatment?

Get current test results using a kit suited to your pool and record the time. The [CDC’s home-pool testing guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/about/home-pool-and-hot-tub-water-treatment-and-testing.html) explains the importance of checking disinfectant and pH. Follow your equipment and chemical labels; do not combine products or guess a dose from water color.

From a safe position, check whether the normal circulation schedule resumed and whether visible baskets or surface debris need attention under the manufacturer’s procedure. Do not open a pressurized filter or handle flooded electrical equipment.

## When should I arrange recovery service?

Keep swimmers out while water quality is unresolved, especially if the bottom cannot be seen clearly. Ask a professional to assess water balance, circulation and filtration together. [Poolburg’s North Texas green-pool recovery resource](https://poolburg.com/green-pool-recovery-north-texas/) is the relevant service link when discoloration persists or the equipment stopped working.

Request a plan that explains the suspected cause, follow-up testing and the conditions for swimming again. A promised completion time without readings is less useful than a documented reassessment.

### Should I shock the pool immediately?

Not by guesswork. Establish readings, pool volume and the product instructions first; a professional can explain which treatment is appropriate.

### Does clear water mean it is safe again?

Appearance alone is not a complete water-quality check. Confirm appropriate test results and follow the treatment professional’s return-to-swimming instructions.
