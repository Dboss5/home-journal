**Cloudiness can involve water balance, sanitation, or filtration.** Appearance alone cannot identify the cause, and adding another product without readings may make the next diagnosis harder.

## Can I still swim?

Keep swimmers out while the cause is unresolved, particularly if the deep-end bottom is not clearly visible. The [CDC's swimming guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/prevention/index.html) tells swimmers to check that the deep-end drain can be seen. Visibility and properly treated water both matter.

Record when the cloudiness started, recent chemical additions, heavy use, or changes in pump operation. [Poolburg's cloudy-water guide](https://poolburg.com/cloudy-pool-water/) is the relevant pool resource for organizing those clues.

## What should happen next?

Get current readings and ask what they indicate before dosing again. [Poolburg's pool water testing guide](https://poolburg.com/pool-water-testing/) explains the testing context; follow your test kit and chemical labels rather than guessing quantities.

If the water stays cloudy, arrange a pool inspection and provide the readings and equipment symptoms. Do not open a pressurized filter or mix chemicals to experiment. Ask how the treatment and circulation will be checked before swimming resumes.
