**Persistent bubbles from the return jets can mean air is entering the circulation system, but the bubbles do not locate the fault.** A brief change after servicing is different from a continuous stream that accompanies weak flow or a pump struggling to prime.

## When do the bubbles appear?

Record whether they occur at startup, after a speed change or throughout normal operation. Note the water level, any visible air under the pump lid and whether a cleaner was recently connected. A video from a safe distance is often more useful than describing the water as simply foamy.

[Poolburg’s return-bubble guide](https://poolburg.com/air-bubbles-from-pool-return-jets/) is the relevant starting point. Do not assume the jet producing the bubbles is where air entered the system.

## What should I avoid doing?

Do not loosen a pump lid, filter clamp or connection while equipment is running or pressurized. Keep clear of electrical parts and do not repeatedly restart a pump that cannot maintain water flow. If equipment runs dry, stop it using the normal accessible control and arrange service.

The [Pentair pool-pump guide](https://www.pentair.com/education-support/water-education-center/pool-and-spa-education/pool-pump-information-guide.html) provides manufacturer context. Your model’s manual determines its operating and service procedures; a general article cannot replace those instructions.

## What should an inspection establish?

Ask the technician to assess the water supply, suction path, accessible seals and operating conditions before recommending parts. If weak returns or a low gauge reading accompany the bubbles, [Poolburg’s low-filter-pressure guide](https://poolburg.com/pool-filter-pressure-too-low/) helps explain the additional symptoms to report.

Request a clear finding and a circulation check after the work. Replacing one seal without confirming the operating result may leave another problem unnoticed.

### Are bubbles and surface foam the same thing?

No. Describe where the bubbles originate and whether foam lingers on the surface. Those observations may lead to different checks.

### Does clear pool water mean the pump is fine?

No. Appearance does not confirm adequate flow. Report persistent bubbles even if the water still looks clear.
