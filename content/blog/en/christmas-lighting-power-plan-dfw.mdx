A Christmas lighting quote should explain more than the roofline design. It should show how the display will receive power and who is responsible when an outlet, product, or lighting section needs attention. Agreeing on that plan before installation helps keep the scope clear.

## Map the display and available power

List the roofline sections, trees, entry features, and paths you want illuminated. Photograph existing outdoor receptacles from a safe position. Note doors, pedestrian routes, and places where water collects so the installer can plan access and cable routing.

Do not open electrical panels or assume separate outlets mean separate circuits. If the power arrangement is uncertain, [Supremacy Service's electrical safety inspections](https://supremacyservice.com/electrical-safety-inspections-dfw/) are a relevant service to discuss before connecting a new display.

## Ask for the product information

Request the product type, intended outdoor use, connection limits, and the installation instructions the provider will follow. Have the installer explain how connections will be protected and how the display will be switched off.

The [CPSC's seasonal lighting guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Household-Electrical-Products/Seasonal-and-Decorative-Lighting-Products) identifies wire size, strain relief, and overcurrent protection as important product safety characteristics. Product appearance alone is not enough to assess those characteristics; keep the supplied labels and instructions with your installation records.

## Define the installation scope

Ask who provides the lights, who owns them afterward, and what is included in the quoted area. Discuss roof access, tree work, timers or controls, and any electrical work that must happen first. An installation booking should not silently become approval for unrelated circuit alterations.

For the display itself, [Supremacy Service's Christmas light installation page](https://supremacyservice.com/christmas-light-installation/) is the appropriate service destination. Request current pricing tied to the agreed design and property access.

## Know who handles a problem

Ask how to report a failed section, what response is included during the season, and whether replacement products are covered. If a protective device repeatedly trips, stop using the affected display and arrange diagnosis. Do not bypass protection or substitute a larger breaker to keep lights on.

Smoke, sparking, or a burning electrical smell requires urgent professional attention. Keep people away from damaged products or wiring.

## Include the end of the season

Agree on removal timing, storage, and any inspection after the lights come down. Confirm how clips and products will be removed and who records damage or missing items. A written installation-and-removal scope is easier to compare than two quotes that only list a bulb count.

Keep the design, product details, approved work, and contact information together for next year. That gives the next installation a reliable starting point instead of relying on memory.
