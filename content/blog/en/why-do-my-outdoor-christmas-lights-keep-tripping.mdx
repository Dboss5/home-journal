**Outdoor Christmas lights can go out because of a ground fault, damaged equipment, an overloaded circuit or another electrical problem.** Rain-related trips and trips after adding decorations are useful clues, but neither pattern is a diagnosis. Do not bypass protection to keep the display on.

## Which device actually tripped?

From a safe, dry position, note whether an outlet’s GFCI, a portable protective device or the panel breaker operated. Photograph accessible labels and record whether other equipment lost power. A timer switching off on schedule is a different problem, so include the timer setting too.

If connectors or equipment are wet, damaged, hot or sparking, keep people away and stop using the display. Do not handle wet plugs or keep pressing reset. Have a qualified professional assess the affected circuit and decoration equipment.

## What should I check in the display plan?

Collect the labels for each type of light string, extension cord and timer. Record how many strings are connected together and which outlet supplies each section. Follow the manufacturer’s connection limits rather than assuming all LED strings can be linked in the same quantity.

The [Electrical Safety Foundation’s outdoor-decoration guidance](https://www.esfi.org/outdoor-decoration-safety-tips/) explains the importance of outdoor-rated equipment and GFCI protection. Keep the display’s electrical assessment separate from its visual design.

### Can I use an unprotected outlet instead?

No. Changing outlets to avoid a protective trip does not correct the underlying problem. Arrange diagnosis and retain the appropriate protection.

### Does a trip after rain mean the lights are ruined?

Not necessarily, but it warrants an assessment of the equipment, connections and exposure. Do not energize wet equipment simply to find out.
