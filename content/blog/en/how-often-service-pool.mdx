Most pool owners fall into one of two camps: they either obsess over it weekly or forget it exists until the water turns green. Both approaches cost money. Here's what actually works.

## The short answer

**Weekly during swim season, bi-weekly in shoulder months, and monthly in winter if you keep it open.** That's it. Everything else is noise.

But the details matter, and they change based on where you live.

## Why weekly matters in summer

When water hits 78°F or above, algae can bloom in under 48 hours. Chlorine burns off faster under direct sun. Your filter runs longer. Everything accelerates.

A weekly service isn't just cleaning — it's **balancing**. That means:

- Testing pH, alkalinity, calcium hardness, and cyanuric acid
- Adjusting chemicals to prevent scale and corrosion
- Skimming, brushing, and vacuuming
- Checking the pump, filter, and skimmer baskets

Skip a week in July and you'll spend two weeks catching up.

## What a good pro actually does

If your pool tech is only dumping chlorine in and leaving, you're not getting what you're paying for. Here's what to expect from a proper service visit:

1. **Test the water** with a real test kit, not strips
2. **Log the readings** so you can see trends over time
3. **Adjust chemicals** based on the readings, not a guess
4. **Clean the surfaces** — brush walls, skim surface, vacuum floor
5. **Inspect equipment** — pump, filter, heater, lights
6. **Report findings** — anything that needs repair or attention

That's the standard. Anything less and you should ask questions.

## The cost question

Weekly service typically runs **$120–$200/month** in most markets. That sounds like a lot until you price out the alternative: a green pool recovery can cost $300–$600, and equipment failures from neglect run into the thousands.

**The math always favors prevention.**

## When to do it yourself

If you have a small pool, low usage, and time on your hands, you can absolutely maintain it yourself. The catch is you need to learn the chemistry — pH, alkalinity, and chlorine management aren't intuitive.

Most DIY pool owners spend their first year fighting the water. If that sounds like a nightmare, hire someone.

## When to call a pro

Call a pro if:

- The water is cloudy and won't clear
- You see algae on the walls or floor
- Your pump is making unusual noises
- You're opening or closing for the season
- You're going on vacation for more than a week
