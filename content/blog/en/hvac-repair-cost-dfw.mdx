Texas summers are brutal on HVAC systems. When yours starts making noise, blowing warm air, or jacking up the electric bill, you need to know two things fast: what's wrong, and what it'll cost to fix.

This guide answers the second question with real numbers from across the DFW metroplex. Rates current as of early 2025.

## The short answer

**AC repair in DFW costs $150–$800** for most issues. **Annual tune-ups run $90–$200. Full system replacement costs $5,000–$12,000** depending on size and efficiency.

That's the range. Here's what actually drives the number.

## What drives the price

**System size.** Bigger homes need bigger systems. A 1,500 sq ft home needs a 2-ton system; a 3,000 sq ft home needs a 4- or 5-ton unit.

**Efficiency rating (SEER).** Higher SEER = higher upfront cost but lower monthly bills. The sweet spot in DFW is usually SEER 15–17.

**Brand.** Trane, Carrier, and Lennox run 10–20% higher than Goodman, Rheem, or Amana. Quality difference is real but modest.

**Time of year.** Emergency summer calls cost 20–40% more than off-season work. Book tune-ups in spring.

**Ductwork condition.** Leaky ducts waste 20–30% of your cooling. If yours are old, budget $1,500–$4,000 for sealing or replacement.

## What to look for in a technician

A good HVAC tech:

- Has NATE certification
- Carries EPA 608 certification for handling refrigerants
- Gives a written estimate before starting work
- Explains what's wrong in plain language
- Offers both repair and replace options with numbers attached

A bad one:

- Diagnoses over the phone
- Won't give a written estimate
- Pushes replacement for a system under 10 years old
- Prices "start at" without a firm number
- Shows up in an unmarked truck

## How to get a fair quote

Ask these questions:

1. What's your diagnostic fee, and do you waive it if I book the repair?
2. Is the estimate written and firm?
3. What's the repair-vs-replace math for this specific issue?
4. Do you offer financing on replacements?
5. What warranty comes with the work?

Good providers answer all five. Bad ones get vague.
