When your air conditioner stops cooling, the first price you hear may be a diagnostic fee—not the cost of putting the system right. Before approving work, ask what the technician found and what the proposed repair will resolve.

This guide helps DFW homeowners compare a repair with another repair, and a replacement with a replacement. Those are different decisions.

## The short answer

Get the diagnostic charge, repair scope, and total before authorizing the repair. If replacement is proposed, request a separate written proposal with equipment model numbers and installation details. A price without those details is difficult to compare.

## Start with the diagnosis

Ask what was tested, which component failed, and whether another problem contributed to the failure. A symptom such as warm air is not itself a diagnosis. Ask what the technician will check after the repair to confirm operation.

## A refrigerant quote needs a clear scope

Ask whether the proposal includes investigation, any identified repair, refrigerant, and post-repair checks. If a provider proposes adding refrigerant without resolving a suspected leak, ask what that achieves and what remains unresolved. Leave refrigerant handling to qualified professionals.

## Repair or replace: compare options, not a fixed rule

A percentage of replacement cost cannot decide the job on its own. Consider the diagnosis, repair history, warranty coverage, comfort problems, and the complete replacement proposal. Ask the provider to explain both options and any uncertainty.

ENERGY STAR identifies frequent repairs, rising energy bills, and comfort problems as reasons to seek a professional assessment. These signs call for investigation; they do not establish that every system needs replacement.

Do not choose capacity from floor area alone. ENERGY STAR recommends evaluating the home, insulation, windows, and duct system; its sizing guidance points homeowners to a Manual J load calculation.

## Maintenance: ask what gets checked

Compare the checklist, number of visits, reporting, and repair discounts in a maintenance agreement. Ask whether filters, cleaning, and additional diagnostic visits are included. A maintenance visit should not be sold as a guarantee that equipment cannot fail.

## Five questions before you approve

1. What failed, and what evidence supports that finding?
2. What is included in the total, and is the diagnostic fee credited?
3. What does the warranty cover, and what remains my responsibility?
4. If I choose replacement, what installation work is excluded?
5. What checks will confirm the completed work performs as intended?
