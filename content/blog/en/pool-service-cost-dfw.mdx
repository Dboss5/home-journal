A monthly fee is useful only when you know what it buys. Two pool-service plans can look similar until one excludes chemicals, bills filter cleaning separately, or counts visits differently.

For a DFW homeowner comparing Poolburg with another provider, the first step is to put both plans on the same scope. Use this guide before signing up or changing providers.

## The short answer

Ask for the recurring fee, the visit schedule, included tasks, and charges outside the plan in writing. Compare the annual total for those same items. A chemical-only visit and a full cleaning visit are different services.

## What changes the estimate

**Debris and access.** Describe nearby trees, attached spas, access restrictions, and the surfaces that need cleaning. Photos can help a provider prepare, but an inspection may still be needed.

**Starting condition.** Ask whether bringing a neglected pool back into service is a separate job. A recovery visit should explain what is being treated and whether follow-up visits are included.

**Chemicals and equipment.** Clarify which chemicals are included and whether the plan has limits. Ask who approves replacement parts and repairs before the technician proceeds.

**Billing structure.** A monthly plan and a per-visit plan need an annual comparison. Do not assume an extra charge is unfair until you have checked what the base plan covers.

## A visit does not replace checks between visits

CDC guidance recommends routinely testing pool disinfectant and pH, including between professional visits. Ask your provider what you should monitor and how to report a change. Follow product directions and keep treatment chemicals secured.

## Equipment repair: separate the work

A pump quote should identify the model, whether the proposal repairs a part or replaces the assembly, installation work, and the warranty. For any equipment replacement, ask what happens if the new unit requires additional plumbing, electrical work, or controls.

<Callout>
  **Before approving an extra:** Ask for the finding, the proposed work, the total, and what happens if you defer it. Agree on an approval limit for work outside the recurring plan.
</Callout>

## Build your own annual budget

Start with the written recurring fee. Add separately quoted filter maintenance and other planned work. Keep repairs as a separate allowance based on your equipment’s condition rather than treating a generic average as an expected bill. Ask whether electricity, water, and consumables are outside the service quote.

## Five questions to send with your request

1. How many visits does the plan include, and what happens after a missed visit?
2. Which cleaning tasks and chemicals are included?
3. What is charged separately, including filter maintenance?
4. Will I receive readings and a record of completed work?
5. Will you obtain approval before repairs or additional treatment?
