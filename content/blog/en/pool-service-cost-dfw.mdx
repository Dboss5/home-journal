If you've tried to find out what pool service actually costs in DFW, you already know the frustration. Every provider says "it depends." Every website hides the price behind a form. You're supposed to book a consultation just to find out if you can afford it.

This guide fixes that. Below are real numbers from across the DFW metroplex — Dallas, Plano, Frisco, McKinney, Allen, Southlake, Fort Worth, and surrounding areas. Rates current as of early 2025.

## The short answer

**Weekly pool service in DFW runs $120–$220 per month.** Bi-weekly service runs $80–$140. One-time deep cleanings cost $250–$450. Equipment repairs start at $150 and can reach $800+ depending on the part.

That's the range. Everything below explains *why* it swings, so you can tell whether a quote you're getting is fair.

## What drives the price up

Four things move the number:

**1. Pool size**
Larger pools need more chemicals and take longer to clean. A 30,000-gallon pool can cost 40–60% more to service than a 15,000-gallon one.

**2. Pool condition**
If your pool is currently green, cloudy, or has visible algae, expect a one-time recovery fee of $200–$500 to get it back to baseline.

**3. Location**
Frisco, Plano, and Southlake tend to run 10–20% higher than Dallas proper. Fort Worth is often 5–15% lower.

**4. Add-ons**
Filter cleaning ($60–$120 each time), tile brushing, and equipment repair are billed separately. Some providers bundle them; others don't.

## What to look for in a provider

Not all pool service is created equal. Here's what separates a good provider from a cheap one:

**They test with real kits.** Not strips. Strips are fine for quick checks, but professional service should use a proper drop-test kit that measures pH, alkalinity, calcium hardness, and cyanuric acid.

**They log readings.** A good technician leaves a report — either paper or in an app — showing what was tested and what was added.

**They're licensed and insured.** In Texas, pool service companies should carry general liability insurance. Ask for proof.

**They're CPO certified.** Certified Pool Operator certification is the industry standard. It means the tech has actually been trained, not just trained on the job by someone else who wasn't.

**They have local references.** Ask for three recent local customers. Any honest provider will share them.

## Red flags

Watch for these:

- **"Price depends on consultation"** — every service is different, but a range should be available up front
- **No written quote** — verbal prices aren't enforceable
- **Door-to-door sales** — legitimate pool companies don't knock on doors
- **Cash only** — no paper trail means no warranty
- **No license or insurance** — huge risk if something goes wrong

<Callout>
  **If a provider won't give you a range over the phone,** that's usually a red flag. Legitimate pool companies can quote a range based on pool size and zip code. If they can't, they're hiding something.
</Callout>

## Is DIY worth it?

Running the numbers honestly: DIY pool ownership saves the service fee but costs you 4–8 hours a month plus the learning curve on water chemistry. Most people who try it spend the first year fighting algae and cloudy water.

If your time is worth more than $20/hour, hire a pro. If you genuinely enjoy the maintenance, DIY can work — just budget for a proper test kit and be ready to learn the chemistry.

## How to get a fair quote

When you ask for a quote, ask these five questions:

1. What's included in the monthly fee?
2. Do you test with a kit or strips?
3. Do you provide written reports?
4. Are you CPO certified and insured?
5. What's your pricing on filter cleaning and equipment repair?

A provider who answers all five clearly is probably a good one. A provider who deflects is probably not.
