**Scratching in an attic is a reason to investigate, but the sound alone cannot identify the animal.** Rodents, squirrels, other wildlife and even a building component can produce noises that carry through a ceiling. Choosing a treatment before identification risks missing the real source.

## Which observations help an inspection?

Record the time, duration and approximate location of the noise from inside the living space. Note whether it follows wind, occurs during the day or night, or appears when the HVAC starts. A short recording may help describe it, but timing is not proof of a species.

From ground level, photograph visible roofline damage or repeated animal activity. Do not climb onto the roof, handle animals or enter an attic with uncertain footing, contaminated material or damaged wiring.

## Why identify the animal before closing a gap?

An opening may be active, may serve more than one route or may have animals still inside. Ask for identification and a removal or exclusion plan before permanent repairs. The [EPA’s rodent identification and prevention guidance](https://www.epa.gov/rodenticides/identify-and-prevent-rodent-infestations) provides context for combining evidence, food-source control and prevention.

## What should an exclusion plan explain?

Request photographs of accessible evidence, the identified entry routes, the sequence of work and how the team will check for remaining activity. Ask which repairs, cleanup and follow-up visits are included rather than assuming one treatment solves every issue.

Do not scatter poison in the attic or cover an opening simply because it looks suspicious. A plan should explain the target animal and how people, pets and non-target wildlife will be protected.

### Does daytime noise prove it is a squirrel?

No. Timing can guide an inspection but is not sufficient identification. Ask what physical evidence supports the finding.

### Should I seal the roofline immediately?

Have active openings assessed first so animals are not trapped inside. Urgent weather damage can still need attention, coordinated with the exclusion professional.
