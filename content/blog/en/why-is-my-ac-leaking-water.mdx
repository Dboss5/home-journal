**Water leaking inside the home is not a normal place for AC condensate to end up.** A blocked drain, damaged pan or another equipment problem may be involved. The stain shows where water appeared, not necessarily where the fault began.

## What should I do first?

If cooling is causing an active indoor leak, stop cooling at the thermostat and arrange service. Keep clear of wet electrical equipment. A sagging ceiling or water near wiring needs urgent professional attention; do not enter an attic to investigate an unsafe area.

Photograph the leak only from a safe position. Note whether it happens during cooling, after shutdown or even when the system has not been running. Record weak airflow, visible ice and recent maintenance. Do not chip ice away or try to force the equipment to keep cooling.

## Why does the drain matter?

Air conditioning removes moisture as it cools. That water needs a functioning route away from the indoor equipment. The [ENERGY STAR maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) includes checking the condensate drain, which makes it an important part of a proper inspection.

Do not pour an unapproved cleaner into an unidentified pipe or bypass a safety switch. A drain line, secondary outlet and other household pipework can be confused without knowing the installation. [Supremacy’s AC repair page](https://supremacyservice.com/air-conditioner-repair/) is the appropriate service starting point for an indoor cooling-related leak.

## What should the technician check?

Ask for findings on the drain route, pan condition, drain supports and any overflow protection. If icing or poor airflow occurred, ask how its cause will be evaluated rather than assuming that clearing water resolves everything.

Use [Supremacy’s air-conditioning service guide](https://supremacyservice.com/air-conditioning/) to discuss the wider system context. Request confirmation that drainage was tested after the work and ask what visible change should prompt another call.

### Is water outside always a problem?

Some installations discharge condensate outdoors normally. A new discharge pattern, an overflow outlet running or indoor staining deserves evaluation.

### Can I keep running the AC with a bucket underneath?

A bucket does not address hidden damage or electrical exposure. Stop the active problem and get the drainage and equipment assessed.
