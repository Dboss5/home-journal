**Returning ants do not automatically mean more spray is the right answer.** The species, food and moisture sources, entry routes and treatment method all affect the next step. A trail disappearing briefly is not proof that the underlying source has been addressed.

## What should I document?

Photograph ants and trails from a safe distance and note the room, time and repeated entry point. Record spills, pet food, nearby moisture and any recent treatment. Keep the service report or product details so the next professional knows what was already used.

Do not handle unfamiliar ants or disturb a nest to identify it. Tell the provider if activity is inside, near food or around areas used by children and pets.

## Why can extra spraying be unhelpful?

The [University of California IPM ant guide](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/ants/) explains why identification and the control method matter. If bait is part of a professional plan, adding an unrelated spray can interfere with that approach. Ask before changing products or removing treatment materials.

Follow the product label and the provider’s instructions. Reducing accessible food and reporting moisture problems can support the plan, but treatment timing and follow-up depend on the identified pest and method.

### Does a clean kitchen guarantee ants will disappear?

No. Food access is only one factor. Entry routes, moisture and nesting locations may also need attention.

### Should I change the bait if ants are still visible?

Ask the provider first. The meaning of activity depends on the treatment method and elapsed time; do not replace a plan with random products.
